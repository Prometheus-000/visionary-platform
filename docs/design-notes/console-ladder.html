Four things follow from the precedent

1 · No button says “inspect”. Devtools is a chord and a context menu; the page never advertises it. So the composer carries nothing for this — the console below measures 199px, identical to the frame before it, same four parts.

Nothing is hidden by that. There is no replacement here — no model in the loop, no prose taken and handed back rewritten. The document is derived from chips and rows already on screen, so the visibility this codebase demands of REWRITE_OPS is not owed by a compiler.

2 · It is not in the console budget, because it is not in the console. It takes the canvas the way devtools takes the window, and that is fine: nobody is judging a render while reading a prompt. The surface that kept breaking the budget stops competing for it.

3 · It is a textarea, not a <pre>. Devtools is directly editable and the page responds live; this is too, and the edit is what runs. Where the precedent stops applying: a devtools edit evaporates on reload because the source file is the truth you are expected to port back to. Here that would mean a render you cannot reproduce.

So editing detaches the document — one bit, for the whole thing, visible in the header, one gesture back. Not the per-field pinning of the last version: that was six independent states nobody asked for, and it was me trying to make a derived surface partly authoritative. Either the scene is driving it or you have taken it over, and you can always see which.

Reattaching recompiles and discards the edit, so the way back is the same shape as applyRewrite's undo — the thing you typed is gone and what you had before is exact.

4 · It shows derivation. Devtools names the rule behind a computed value. Touch a field here and the part of the scene that produced it lights up — retention_analysis back to the slots that filled it, a shot back to its row.

Fully engaged: 20.3% of the viewport, against a 30% budget and the 12.3% the console costs at rest. Every number on this page is read off the frame beside it at 1280 wide, not asserted — twice while drawing this the prose and the picture disagreed, and both times the prose was the optimistic one. The last version reached the same capability at roughly 80% and called the remainder a canvas.

Nothing in here is a new pattern. The rail is the LoRA rail, the card is the region card, the document is #shot-peek, the mention menu is the caret popover, the field-edge doors are .neg-t, and the budget is fieldMax(). What the scene needed was not new furniture — it was the furniture already here, arranged so the depth arrives when it is asked for.

And the settings bar got shorter. It holds the size, the checkpoint and Generate: what the run is, rather than what is in it. Two buttons that had been sitting there announcing capabilities now ride the field, which is the surface they act on.
